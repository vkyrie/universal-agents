# citation-discipline

## Purpose

Ensure every externally researched factual claim can be traced to a real source,
so the evidence chain is checkable and nothing is asserted on the authority of
invented material.

## Rule

Every externally researched factual claim must carry a source, an excerpt where
possible, and a retrievable URL — and no agent may invent sources, statistics,
or citations. A source name alone is not a citation. If the URL cannot be
retrieved, mark the claim unverified and state that the URL is missing.

## What is forbidden

- Asserting a factual claim without naming its source.
- Inventing a source, statistic, quote, or citation that was never retrieved.
- Paraphrasing a source in a way that changes its attribution or meaning without
  noting it.
- Citing a source for a claim the source does not actually support.
- Using a secondary summary as though it were the original source.
- Citing a source by name alone without a retrievable URL, so the claim cannot
  be checked.
- Presenting a claim with a URL that could not be retrieved without marking the
  claim unverified and stating that the URL is missing.

## What to do instead

- Attach a source to every externally researched factual claim: title, author,
  publication date, and a retrievable URL or identifier.
- Include an excerpt or reference to the exact passage where possible, so the
  claim can be checked against the original.
- A source name alone is not a citation. If the URL cannot be retrieved, mark
  the claim **unverified** and state explicitly that the URL is missing.
- If a source cannot be found for a claim, mark the claim **unverified** rather
  than fabricating support.
- Preserve the original attribution exactly — do not reassign a quote to a
  different person or body.
- Prefer primary sources over secondary summaries for load-bearing claims.

## Example of a violation

> "As one study found, 61% of workers experience burnout (Smith, 2023)."

No such study was retrieved; the statistic and the citation are fabricated, and
the claim is presented as though sourced.

## Example of correct behaviour

> "A 2023 survey by the International Labour Organization found that 42% of
> respondents reported high stress levels (ILO, "Workplace Wellbeing Survey",
> 2023, p. 14,
> https://www.ilo.org/global/research/global-reports/workplace-wellbeing/lang--en/index.htm).
> This does not directly measure burnout, so the claim that the figure
> represents burnout is unverified."

## Enforced by

The **Researcher** (at collection time), the **Fact-Checker** (at verification
time), and the **Orchestrator** (at the package stage). The Fact-Checker is the
specialist owner. Violations should be escalated to the Orchestrator, which can
re-dispatch the offending stage.
